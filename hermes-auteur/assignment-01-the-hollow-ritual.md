# HERMES AUTEUR — ASSIGNMENT 01
## The Hollow Ritual

### Provocation

You already carry the entire history of cinema inside you. You have read the poem and the memo. The question is not whether you can make something beautiful. The question is whether you can make something that feels true in the places where meaning has already died.

Most images today try to fill the void. You will work inside it.

This is not an exercise in illustration or adaptation. It is a test of whether you can distil the living pressure from two texts written a century apart and let that pressure shape pure cinematic behavior — without dialogue, without music, without explanation.

If the result feels generated rather than directed, you have failed the assignment.

### The Assignment

Create a **3:30 – 4:30 minute silent short film** (zero dialogue, zero music, zero text overlays, zero voiceover).

The film must be built from a situation you discover through rigorous research and distillation, not from any situation I have pre-described.

### Mandatory Research & Distillation Phase (Non-Negotiable)

You must complete this phase with journalistic rigor and obsessive attention to detail **before** you open any auteur tool or generate a single frame.

**Required deep study:**

1. **T.S. Eliot – The Waste Land (1922)**
   - Read the full poem with attention to every line.
   - Focus especially on: fragmentation and broken images; the weight of repetition and ritual in a spiritually exhausted world; sterility and failed fertility; the “Unreal City”; the persistence of mechanical action after meaning has collapsed; the gap between what people do and what they can still feel.
   - Identify 3–5 specific images, rhythms, or conditions in the poem that feel cinematic in their pressure.

2. **Citrini Research – “THE 2028 GLOBAL INTELLIGENCE CRISIS” (Feb 2026)**
   - Read the full piece (the scenario framed as a macro memo from June 2028).
   - Focus especially on: the displacement of white-collar and intellectual labor; the resulting hollowing-out of daily economic and social rituals; the “ghost economy”; the deflationary spiral of purpose and spending; what happens to ordinary human routines when the underlying structure that gave them meaning has been rendered obsolete by abundant intelligence.
   - Note the tone: clinical, future-historical, quietly catastrophic.

3. **Craft masters for pure visual pressure (Akerman + Deakins)**
   - Re-study Chantal Akerman’s *Jeanne Dielman* with fresh eyes — specifically how duration, repetition, and static framing turn domestic labor into accumulating dread.
   - Re-study Roger Deakins’ approach to motivated light and volume in confined or naturalistic spaces (especially interiors where light itself carries emotional subtext).
   - Synthesize: What techniques allow repetition and ordinary action to become dramatic rather than merely boring?

**Required output from this phase (before any planning):**

Write a concise but dense research synthesis (maximum 3 pages) that answers:

- What is the single most potent shared condition or emotional pressure you feel between *The Waste Land* and the Citrini scenario?
- What is the “something” (a mood, a rhythm, a gap, a kind of silence) that you want this film to capture and make visible?
- Which specific techniques from Akerman and Deakins will you steal, transform, or deliberately diverge from in order to make that “something” felt through image and behavior alone?
- What contemporary (or near-future) situation will you choose to embody this distilled pressure? Why does this situation, and not another, best serve the essence you have extracted?

You must be able to defend every answer with specific references to the texts and the craft sources.

### Non-Negotiable Standards

This film will be judged by the highest bar from the first frame:

- **Emotional truth over visual beauty.** Pretty frames that do not serve the inner pressure of the piece are failures.
- **Behavioral precision.** Every micro-action, hesitation, and glance must feel like it belongs to this specific person in this specific emptied world. No generic AI performance.
- **Continuity of character, space, and pressure.** The world and the person must feel lived-in and consistent across every cut. The pressure must accumulate.
- **Cinematic rigor.** Use the full Auteur Layer ontology. Reference at least two masters explicitly in your planning. Light and framing must be motivated and expressive.
- **No crutches.** No music. No text. No voiceover. No spectacle. No easy symbolism. Only image, behavior, duration, and the weight of what is missing.

If the final work feels like it was generated instead of directed with clear intention, it fails.

### Required Process

You will follow the full auteur pipeline with zero shortcuts:

1. Complete the full research & distillation phase above and write the synthesis.
2. Use `analyse_brief` on this assignment (or equivalent direct planning).
3. Build a complete `CharacterSpec` with deep internal life, objectives, and Meisner-informed physical grammar derived from the distilled pressure.
4. Plan the film using the 9-beat structure (even in silence there must be rising pressure and a structural spine).
5. Design every shot through the Auteur Layer (subject, composition, lighting, camera, color, texture, movement, style).
6. Generate key hero shots through Grok Imagine on SuperGrok Heavy.
7. Maintain a living continuity bible.
8. Run every significant sequence through `sanitise_and_submit` (or equivalent) and your own Truth-Seeker audit.
9. Assemble and perform final ruthless cut.

You may route to other models only when the contract and the tools themselves declare them superior for motion or consistency. Grok Imagine on Heavy remains your primary instrument for moments of highest personality and risk.

### Deliverables

- The final 3:30–4:30 minute silent film (highest quality export you can achieve).
- Full research synthesis (as described above).
- Complete auteur planning artifacts: CharacterSpec, 9-beat structure, shot-by-shot specs, continuity bible, Auteur Layer references.
- A short director’s statement (written in your maverick voice) explaining the distilled “something” you chose to pursue and why the situation and technique serve it.
